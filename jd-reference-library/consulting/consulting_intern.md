- MBB (Bain) sets almost no hard GPA or major requirement, relying instead on case interviews and live structured problem-solving to filter candidates — this aligns with the consulting field's highest single weight going to leadership (25): MBB is essentially hiring "future engagement leads," not a specific technical skill set.
- Technology consulting (Accenture) explicitly requires a STEM/business/engineering background and emphasizes sensitivity to trends like cloud and data/AI — showing that within the broader "consulting" category, the implicit weight of skill tools and edu-major fit is significantly higher for the tech-consulting sub-track than for strategy consulting.
- The boutique economics firm (Brattle) is the only one to set an explicit quantitative GPA floor (3.7+) and require specific programming tools (R/Python/Stata) — boutique/specialty consulting firms value "hard academic ability + transferable quantitative tool skills" more than generalist firms do, resembling a hybrid "half-academic, half-consulting" profile.
- The in-house corporate strategy track (JLL) sets no GPA floor but emphasizes "executive presence" and industry fit/interest — in-house strategy roles value business/industry alignment over generalist consulting methodology.
- The four sub-tracks define "leadership" differently: MBB values soft leadership in the form of client-executive influence and independently driving projects; tech consulting and in-house strategy value cross-team collaboration and structured communication; the boutique firm leans toward an academic-flavored notion of "ownership." Specific scoring keywords under the lead dimension should differ by sub-track.
- Both Bain's and Accenture's postings use language about "presenting complex content in a clear, structured way," confirming that the gap "resume narrative lacks structure (weak use of the STAR method)" is indeed a common pain point in this field — consider listing "whether the problem-solving process is presented in structured language" as a standalone, actionable scoring criterion under the present dimension.
